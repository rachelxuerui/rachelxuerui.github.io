• 013
    Installation in Venice

Still Life is a site-specific, outdoor installation of a working farm that grows, harvests, and distributes food in Venice. It is the first farm on the main island in the city's history. The project was conceived with artist Linda Goode Bryant for the 2026 Venice Biennale.

We could tell you that the farm is lifted off the ground to create shaded areas where Linda's films can be projected; to improve the ergonomics of farmers; to display the crops at eye-level as if they were art objects; or to protect them from flood and rodents. All of this is true, but we also just loved that a farm the size of a tennis court should hover like a magic carpet.

The structure is made of cross-laminated timber panels harvested and fabricated less than 100 miles from the site. Thin steel columns carry their loads to the ground. While designed for Venice, it is engineered for a life after the Biennale. The modular CLT panels are sized for transport by cargo, and their assembly is entirely mechanically fastened, allowing the installation to be disassembled, shipped, and reassembled elsewhere.

Under the farm, multi-media assemblages made of farm tools and AV equipment broadcast a Venice that Biennale visitors rarely experience: footage of the exhibition grounds overgrown with weeds before opening, ivy-clad buildings at the edges of the sites, and audio recordings from a quieter Venice of songbirds, olive trees, and still waters.

As of , the project is on view at the Venice Biennale, running from May 9 — November 21, 2026.
